## Why This Space Exists

Over five years of consultancy research across technology, automotive, financial services, and FMCG, I have repeatedly observed a tension: commercial product cycles often demand rapid generalisation, while the most critical human problems demand patient, contextual understanding.

When designing technology for human beings—particularly those navigating chronic or fluctuating health challenges—standard assumptions about linear habit formation, daily adherence, and uniform energy levels collapse.

This website serves as my independent research space: a place to question assumptions, review existing literature, and document structured inquiries at the intersection of human behaviour, health conditions, and technology.

## Core Commitments of This Publication

### 1. Separation of Claims
Every article on this platform clearly delineates between:
* Peer-reviewed empirical literature.
* Qualitative exploratory findings.
* Conceptual design hypotheses and personal reflections.

I write as an Insights Manager and UX researcher examining human systems, behavioural patterns, and service interactions.

### 2. Focusing on Everyday Realities Over Idealised Personas
Standard user personas often assume predictable days: 8 hours of focused work, reliable sleep, and surplus cognitive capacity for complex onboarding. For individuals managing fluctuating health, capacity is a variable, not a constant. This publication prioritises understanding those variable realities.

### 3. Critical Assessment of Technology
Technology is not an unalloyed good. In many instances, digital tracking tools inadvertently impose additional cognitive labour on already exhausted individuals. We must rigorously question when digital interventions support autonomy versus when they become another unmanageable chore.
